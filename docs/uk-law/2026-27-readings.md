## Notes on the readings (2026-10-03)

**One expected verdict does not hold: auto-repair-shops.** The multipliers guidance lists "Garages" as a use within scope and says a garage doing MOT and repair work serves visiting members of the public. The limits that still apply are in the same guidance: the premises must be "wholly or mainly" used for the qualifying purpose and open to the public for it, and they must be occupied. A street vehicle repair workshop that meets those tests is within scope of the retail, hospitality and leisure multipliers, and each council decides: "it will be the responsibility of LAs to determine whether a hereditament meets the legislative definition of RHL and therefore qualifies for the RHL multipliers" (paragraph 5). The expected verdict in the second table (no) must be decided before any figure that gives vehicle repair the national multipliers is written.

Decided 2026-10-03: the page governs. The profit model gives auto-repair-shops the retail, hospitality and leisure multipliers (38.2p below 51,000, 43p from 51,000); the council's "wholly or mainly" test is the caveat a page states.

**Pages named in the plan that do not state the value (the value matches; only the source differs).**
- Marginal relief fraction (row 2): the Marginal Relief guidance has no fraction, no formula and no mention of 3/200; it points to a calculator. The fraction for the year starting 1 April 2026 is on the HMRC rates and allowances page. The manual page CTM03910 tabulates 2023 to 2025 only. The statute (Corporation Tax Act 2010, section 18B(3), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/4/section/18B) says the fraction is "set by Parliament for the financial year", so the rates and allowances page is the page that states it for 2026.
- Multipliers (row 3): the named guidance page has none of 43.2p, 48p, 38.2p or 43p, only "5p below their national equivalents" (43.2 less 5 is 38.2, and 48 less 5 is 43, as printed elsewhere).
- Basic rate band (row 6): 37,700 is not on the income tax rates page, which gives the band as £12,571 to £50,270. It is printed on the HMRC rates and allowances page.
- Employment Allowance amount (row 10): not on the eligibility page. The director rule is worded there as "liable for secondary Class 1 National Insurance", while the plan words it as the only employee paid above the secondary threshold; I read these as the same rule, since employer contributions start above the secondary threshold.
- Employer pension minimum of 3% (row 12): not on the regulator's earnings thresholds page. The regulator's 2025 to 2026 table shows the same three figures as 2026 to 2027, but the page does not use the word unchanged.
- SDLT discount rate and the £5,000,000 band edge (row 15): not on the leasehold guidance page, whose worked example only implies the bands.

**Not stated in one sentence: small business rate relief at the retail, hospitality and leisure multiplier (row 5).** The pages quoted in that row fit together and a council states it outright, but no GOV.UK page states in so many words that relief applies at the 38.2p multiplier. The relief page's examples (50% at £13,500 and 33% at £14,000) fit a straight line, (15,000 minus rateable value) divided by 3,000; the page does not print that formula.

**Welsh LTT (row 16):** the rates page was last updated on 10 December 2024 and its table has no end date. The written statement on the 2026-27 draft Budget says the rates and thresholds stay unchanged; the final Budget was not read.
