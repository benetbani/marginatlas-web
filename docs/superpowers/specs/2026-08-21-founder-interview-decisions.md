## 3. The specific replacements

| What a visitor sees today | What replaces it |
|---|---|
| "Your market: 69.7 million people" on a London coffee page | **People per existing shop.** City population divided by shops already trading. Comparison built in, varies by trade and city |
| A pay-by-role table printing identical chef / server / porter pay on every cafe, bar, bakery and restaurant | **Wages as a share of takings.** Already held, comparable anywhere, and the figure an owner actually needs |
| "The same business nearby": four cities, each the takings times a fixed multiplier | **Only cities the atlas actually knows.** Sometimes two, sometimes six, always true |
| A severity mark reading "rare" on all twenty trades | **Derive it from the figures behind it.** High rent makes a rent risk serious. Must differ between a London restaurant and a Warsaw one |
| Search engines handed "bottom 10% ~ X, top 10% ~ Y" under a key meaning *measured*, built from a fixed 0.25x / 3.4x | **Stop sending it.** Copy what the newer page already does |

## 4. The four never-filled sections — all four authorised

1. How this place compares with nearby ones
2. Visitors against residents as customers
3. Where the money goes, cost by cost
4. **Street level footfall** **[HIS CALL]**

**On footfall.** I flagged that outside a few dozen wealthy cities it may not be
buildable honestly, and that rulebook rule 21 (does it survive Kinshasa, Dhaka,
Tirana, La Paz) says a section that breaks there is wrong. He ruled build it
everywhere, and then resolved the honesty problem himself: **build the estimate
from things the atlas genuinely knows in every city** — businesses per street,
density of the surrounding area — and **print it clean**, like every other
figure. That is a defensible model rather than a decorative one.

## 5. The spread, and the one reconciliation left open

Two answers met awkwardly and were reconciled in the last round.

- **Q34:** the spread rule that wins is the one **measured from real businesses**.
- **Q37:** but the big figure must **always** be a range, so **widen it**: where
  this trade's own firms are not held, build the spread from **similar
  businesses elsewhere**.

**The resolution, and it is the important line:** *a spread is never a multiplier
of the typical figure. It is always built from real businesses — this trade's if
the atlas holds them, the family's if it does not.* The fixed 0.25x / 3.4x dies
either way. Nine competing spread rules collapse to two, and both are grounded.

> **FLAGGED, NOT DECIDED.** Under R2 a Band whose *shape is invented* carries a
> mark. A family-derived band's shape is real but borrowed. My working reading:
> **mark a band only where its shape came from a formula, never where it came
> from real firms.** This has not been ruled on and should be put to him.
