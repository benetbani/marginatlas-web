# Page content map (founder section quiz, 2026-06-11)

PURE CONTENT, not design. What each page TYPE should contain, in reading order.
Tags: [keep] existing, [new] add, [modify] change an existing section, [sub] small subsection.
Standing rules baked in: like-for-like comparisons only (never mix business x geography);
real data or honest self-omit (never fabricate); plain human copy; nothing a visitor could
find much better elsewhere.

---

## BUSINESS PAGE (one business in one place, e.g. "restaurants in California") — the core

1. Masthead: business + place + the headline number (revenue / what the owner keeps) [keep]
2. One-line honest verdict, plain English (e.g. "thin-margin, rent-sensitive; works if you own the space") [new sub] (ONLY if written sharply)
3. The story in plain words (the narrative) [keep]
4. The revenue picture: typical and the spread (low / median / high) [keep]
5. "In plain terms": revenue translated to tangible units ("about 320 coffees a day") [new sub]
6. Where the money goes: plain "for every $100 of revenue, $X food, $Y staff..." [modify] (replaces the technical waterfall)
7. What the owner takes home [keep, paywall-gated]
8. What it takes to break even: the survival-line revenue [new sub]
9. Who works here and what they're paid: wages by role for this place [new]
10. What it costs to open one here: itemized startup cost with a total [new]
11. When the money comes in: seasonality / busy vs slow months [new]
12. Your realistic first year: ramp-up, break-even timing, year-one take-home [modify] (upgrades the light "if you opened today")
13. The same business a short drive away: 2-3 nearby comparable places, like-for-like [new sub]
14. Related / nearby business pages [keep]

REJECTED here: competition density ("how crowded"); "why these fail" failure modes; a measured-vs-estimated data-trust marker.

---

## COUNTRY PAGE (e.g. Germany) — a standalone decision surface

1. Hero: country + photo [keep]
2. The decisive read: setting up and running a business here [keep]
   - business tax, cost to register, payroll tax on staff [keep]
   - How long it takes to get going: steps + days of red tape, not just cost [new sub]
3. How hard it is to hire here: workforce availability + staff cost [new]
   - The wage floor + what people really earn: minimum wage + typical pay [new sub]
4. How this country compares to its neighbours: like-for-like vs comparable neighbours on tax / pay / setup [new]
5. Cities of the country: best + worst city (on real economics) [keep]
6. Character: culture, government, demographics [keep]
7. Related countries [keep]

---

## CITY PAGE (e.g. London)

1. Masthead: city + Business Climate Score [keep]
2. The board: metro population, average wealth, average salary, cost of living, etc. [keep]
3. Who the local customer is: spending power (income, net wealth, how freely they spend) [new]
4. What shop and office space costs: commercial rent character across the city [new]
5. Tourist money vs local money: visitor-vs-resident demand split [new] (ALWAYS add, founder)
6. What an owner keeps across the everyday trades [keep]
7. The best areas to set up: which neighbourhood suits which business [new]
8. Neighbourhoods: the drilled-down districts (clickable, real flavor) [keep]
9. How the city is changing: growth / direction, real trend only [new, smaller]
10. Rival + peer cities [keep]

---

## INDUSTRY PAGE (a whole business type across places, e.g. bakeries)

1. Hero: the business type [keep]
2. What a typical one looks like: the average operator (size, staff, revenue) [new]
3. Where this business earns the most: ranked across COMPARABLE places only, never mixing geographies [new]
4. The cost structure / how the economics work [keep]
5. Links into the places + business pages [keep]

REJECTED here: small-vs-large "by size" economics; "is this trade growing or shrinking" (trend too thin).

---

## NEIGHBOURHOOD PAGE (one district, e.g. the Marais)

1. Hero: the district [keep]
2. What thrives here and why: the businesses that do well + the real reason (crowd, rent, foot traffic) [new]
3. Who lives and shops here: real make-up + who the customers are [new]
4. How pricey it is to operate here: rent / price tier [new]
5. How this area compares to the ones next to it: like-for-like vs adjacent districts [new]
6. The businesses here (neighbourhood industries) [keep]

---

## LEARN PAGE ("how much does a X make" explainers)

1. The question + the headline answer [keep]
2. A worked example: a real sample P&L (revenue minus each cost = what's left) [new]
3. The explanation in plain words [keep]
4. Other businesses worth a look: related trades to branch to [new]
5. Link to the real live benchmarks [keep] (after the US wrong-industry bug is fixed)

REJECTED here: "common mistakes" (too generic).

---

## COMPARE PAGE (two places or two businesses, side by side)

1. The two sides + the headline [keep]
2. The head-to-head numbers [keep]
3. Where each one wins: balanced strengths, honest trade-offs, never "this one is bad" [new]

REJECTED here: "which is easier to start" (risks tipping into a ranking).

# BRAND & EXPERIENCE LAYER (round 2, 2026-06-11)

How every page is WRITTEN and FELT, on top of the content above. North star: a visitor
leaves each page feeling "I finally understand this, and I can trust it."

## Voice & point of view
- Voice: a sharp friend who has the numbers (warm + plain-spoken + clearly expert).
- Has honest OPINIONS, always backed by the numbers ("this is a hard business").
- Speaks to "you" throughout.
- Dry, occasional wit; never jokey, never gimmicky.
- No metaphors: plain, literal language.
- One plain term for the core number everywhere: "what the owner keeps."

## Honesty / trust (the moat)
- Quietly on the reader's side: help you avoid a costly mistake, never sell optimism.
- Admit gaps honestly ("we don't have reliable rent data here yet").
- A plain REALITY-CHECK hard-truth line on business pages.
- A humble "see something off? flag it" on every page.
- Demonstrate breadth via the map + real examples; NO vanity coverage counter.
- A subtle "last checked" freshness date. Method lives on the dedicated methodology page (not per-page links).

## Signature recurring moves (the "Atlas way")
- THE THROUGH-LINE = a recurring "the honest take" box on every page type (the brand's heartbeat). 
- "Who this is right for / wrong for" read.
- "In plain terms" tangible-units translation (BUSINESS PAGES ONLY).
- NOT a single highlighted "hero number" per page (numbers treated equally).
- Consistent SECTION NAMES across page types. Cities remain the ONLY scored entity.
- One consistent authority line site-wide.

## Helping people decide
- Answer-first: bottom line up top, then the detail.
- A 3-question "should you?" gut-check on business pages.
- A light, honest "what to do next" nudge (never a pushy funnel).
- Reader-type personalization (owner / investor / curious): DEFERRED to later.

## Interactive layer (a tool, not a page)
- A "make it yours" adjustable calculator on business pages (build on /calculator).
- Save + track a shortlist of businesses/places.
- One-tap "compare this to..." from anywhere.
- "Watch" a business/place and get told when its numbers change.

## Storytelling & delight
- A short human NARRATIVE per page (the story behind the numbers).
- Real, sourced anecdotes ONLY (never invented; skip where none exist).
- A relevant "from the notebook" link per page.
- Surface a surprising-but-true fact when the data genuinely has one.
- "The one thing to remember" closing line on BIG pages only.
- A light, careful human sign-off. Craft over gimmicks.

## Vision / franchise
- A light "vs the world" global anchor where the comparison is honest.
- Weave the flagship annual benchmarks report through the site (authority + lead magnet).
- A light human presence ("from the Atlas team"); not a faceless institution.

# DESIGN, ALIVENESS & ORIGINALITY LAYER (round 3, 2026-06-12)

How pages are STRUCTURED, MOVE, and stay ONE OF A KIND.

## Build rule (architecture-first)
- COUNTRY / INDUSTRY / CITY new sections: build the skeleton, use labeled `N/A` / placeholder where data is missing, fill later. Do NOT hand-fill data now.
- BUSINESS/CELL, NEIGHBORHOOD, LEARN, COMPARE: real data now.

## Visual hierarchy
- Eye hits FIRST: the answer in one line + the key number.
- Pages are FULL and generous (show everything, not minimal), kept calm by organization, not by hiding content.
- Above the fold on a business page: the bottom line + one or two key numbers, nothing else.
- Data form: a MIX. Prose for the story, cards for headline numbers, tables only for true like-for-like comparisons.

## Long pages
- Sticky in-page jump nav.
- Only the DEEPEST/technical sections collapsed by default (everything else visible).
- The "honest take" box sits right AFTER the headline numbers.

## Numbers & charts
- Headline numbers: confident but not flashy (clear anchor, not a giant hero stat).
- Charts ONLY when the shape matters (a distribution or a trend); otherwise just the number.
- A subtle count-up on the HERO number only.
- ALWAYS show the spread, at ~7 gradations ("7 scales"), not just a single typical number.

## Aliveness / motion (restrained)
- Gentle, fast fade-in as sections scroll in.
- A real freshness ("last checked") signal; NEVER fake "X people viewing."
- The world map has a subtle living quality.
- Motion budget: restrained. Every animation serves clarity, never decoration.

## SUB-TYPE SWITCHING (signature feature)
- A sub-niche click-switch on business pages: restaurants -> pizzeria / kebab / sushi / fine-dining.
- A venue-context switch: high street / mall / airport / station, where the economics genuinely differ.
- Depth: a curated handful per category (the ones that really differ), not every variant.
- Placement: at the TOP, by the business name, reframing the whole page below it.

## Intricacies (the moat)
- Special economic zones (e.g. Dubai free zones) surfaced where they change the economics.
- Captive-venue economics (airport / mall premium) called out.
- Honest impact of corruption / the informal (cash) economy, where it materially matters and we can be factual.
- A "what locals know that outsiders don't" edge per place.

## Raw operator voices (originality engine)
- "What operators actually say": curated, SOURCED real quotes from community sources, lightly framed. Never raw dumps, never fabricated.
- One contrarian / non-obvious insight per page.
- PARALLEL PROJECT: build a raw-perspectives PIPELINE (scrape Reddit / forums + AI deep research -> curated takes per category / place). Its own infra, runs continuously, feeds the whole site.

## New page types unlocked
- Sub-niche pages (e.g. pizzerias across places) -- also strong SEO.
- Venue-type pages (airport businesses, mall businesses).
- Special-zone pages (e.g. Dubai free zones), where significant.
- Scenario pages ("opening a cafe in Lisbon as a foreigner") -- LATER / lower priority.
- Cross-cutting THEME pages (businesses that thrive in tourist / college / oil towns).

## Language
- Practical OPERATOR language, never corporate/consultant ("what you'll clear after rent and staff," not "EBITDA margin optimization").
- Real operator units: covers, tickets, footfall, plates.
- Plain local names: "kebab shop," not "limited-service ethnic restaurant" (also what people search).
- A light, plain glossary for the few unavoidable terms.

## The standard
- Every page must carry at least ONE genuinely unique thing (data point or perspective) nobody else has. If it has nothing unique, it is not done.
- A "myth vs reality" element where the data supports the bust.
- Ambition: RELENTLESS originality, held on every page.
