# Section analysis: City (a metro, e.g. London)

## Who is on this page and what they came for

The City page is mid-funnel: visitors arrive from a country page narrowing down ("UK is interesting, but where?"), from search ("is London good for opening a cafe?"), or laterally from a peer city. **P1 the prospective owner** is the dominant persona, in scouting mode: deciding whether this metro is a place worth committing capital and years to before they ever look at a specific trade. **P5 the curious/SEO arrival** lands on "how good is London for business" and wants a satisfying, shareable verdict. **P3 the investor/analyst** and **P4 the advisor** use it for a fast market read on a metro they're underwriting or briefing a client on. The single question this page must answer: **"Is this metro a friendly, deep market to open a small business in, what kind of customer am I selling to, and what will the place itself (rent, footfall, competition) do to my odds?"** It is a market briefing built around one verdict (the Business Climate Score), not a trade-level P&L: that lives one click down on the cell page.

## Section-by-section audit

### Global navbar + Breadcrumb + world-map masthead band (chrome)
- **Job (T2):** orient and let the user move (up to country, down to cell/neighbourhood, sideways to peers); P1-P5. The breadcrumb is load-bearing wayfinding on a deeply nested site.
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Useful, non-negotiable chrome. The world-map motif is decorative but cheap and on-brand.
- **Cringe check (T3):** The world-map motif risks reading as generic SaaS hero-texture. Keep it at very low opacity and never repeat it lower; it earns its keep only as a faint identity cue, not a "feature."
- **Best visual (T4):** Standard navbar + crumb is correct. No alternative.
- **Redundancy/Depth/Honesty:** None. Fine.
- **VERDICT: KEEP.**

### §1 Hero + Business Climate Score
- **Job (T2):** Deliver the one-number verdict on the metro and the sentence it generates; P1, P5 above all (P3/P4 get the at-a-glance read).
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Useful, and it is the entire reason the page exists. The score + verdict sentence + ScoreBand with peer ticks is the most decision-relevant thing on the site for a scouting operator. The three quiet stats (population, salary, visitors) are good context anchors.
- **Cringe check (T3):** The single biggest cringe/credibility risk on the whole page: a confident "78/100" that a reader suspects is made up. The defense is the verdict sentence doing real work ("strong... if you can fund a slow start") and the honest-take immediately below. Sharpen by making the score caption name the *one tension* that produced the number ("deep demand, expensive ground") rather than a generic "strong on the balance." It already does this; hold that bar for every city.
- **Best visual (T4):** Big Newsreader number + 0-100 ScoreBand with peer ticks is the right pick. Debated alternatives: (a) a radial gauge alone, rejected because a lone radial gives no peer context and reads as a vanity dial; (b) a bar-vs-peers only, rejected because it buries the headline number. The chosen combo (number for the headline moment, band for the "where does it sit" context) is best. The optional re-skinned radial should stay optional and probably be cut (see Cut/Merge). The peer ticks on the band are the masterstroke: they pre-empt "compared to what?" before it's asked.
- **Redundancy (T5):** The peer ticks here partially pre-empt §10 peers. That is fine: hero is the glance, §10 is the drill-down. **Depth (T6):** add a one-line "what moves this score" so the number isn't a black box (the honest-take partly covers this, but the hero should hint). **Honesty (T7):** strong: "Same 0 to 100 scale, only cities are scored" is exactly right; thin-city softening to a break-in chip is the correct guardrail.
- **VERDICT: KEEP.** (Minor reform: caption always names the tension; consider dropping the optional radial.)

### §2 The honest take
- **Job (T2):** The editorial conscience: the one paragraph that tells a P1 the truth the score can't ("rewards the capitalised, punishes the under-funded"). Serves P1 and P5 most.
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Highly useful and the brand's whole differentiator versus a stats dashboard. This is what makes the page feel written by someone who knows the city, not generated.
- **Cringe check (T3):** Biggest risk is generic truisms ("location matters", "manage your costs") that read as AI filler. The London copy mostly avoids this ("pick the right street and the right price, not just the right idea"). Rule to enforce: every bullet must be a claim that is *falsifiable and specific to this metro*, never a platitude that would be true of any city.
- **Best visual (T4):** A calm accent panel with a verdict line + 3 bullets is right. No chart wanted; this is prose-as-signal. Correct.
- **Redundancy (T5):** Slight overlap with §11 one-thing (both are editorial verdict). Distinct enough: §2 is the framing up front, §11 is the warm last word. **Depth (T6):** strong already. **Honesty (T7):** london-exemplar, verdict-only elsewhere; honest.
- **VERDICT: KEEP.**

### §3 Who the local customer is (income spread)
- **Job (T2):** Tell the operator *who they're selling to*: how deep and how skewed the local wallet is. P1 (will my price point work here?), P3 (demand quality).
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Genuinely useful and under-appreciated. "Median income $52K but a long top tail to $140K+" is exactly the input a would-be premium-restaurant or budget-cafe owner needs. The RangeStrip showing the *spread*, not just an average, is the insight: a city's tail is where premium trades live.
- **Cringe check (T3):** Risk: two stats (median income + net wealth per adult) feel like generic almanac facts if not tied to "so what." Sharpen by adding a one-line read: "a broad middle plus a deep luxury tail, so both value and premium concepts can find a base." Net-wealth-per-adult is the weakest stat (abstract to an operator); consider swapping for something more operator-relevant (see below).
- **Best visual (T4):** The RangeStrip (p10-median-p90) is the best possible visual for *distribution* and far better than a single average number. Alternative debated: a small histogram (rejected, too busy and pretends to precision); a single "median" stat (rejected, hides the tail that is the whole point). RangeStrip wins decisively.
- **Redundancy (T5):** None on-page. **Depth (T6):** could deepen with a "what this means for pricing" line. **Honesty (T7):** real spread for London only, self-omits elsewhere with the two stats still carrying it: good honest degradation.
- **VERDICT: KEEP.** (Reform: replace or supplement "net wealth per adult" with a more operator-legible second stat, e.g. disposable income or cost-of-living-adjusted spending power; add a one-line "so what" read.)

### §4 What space costs
- **Job (T2):** Warn the operator that rent is the first line of the budget here, expressed as a *character* not a quote. P1 above all.
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Useful in intent (rent is the #1 killer of small business margins), but currently *thin*: a single index number "132 vs 100 baseline" with a caution. An operator's real question is "how much will a lease actually cost me, roughly?" and a relative index against an unstated national baseline doesn't move a decision much. It's the page's weakest data section.
- **Cringe check (T3):** "132 against a 100 baseline" risks reading as fabricated-precision-with-no-meaning. Sharpen by anchoring the index to something the reader feels: "rents here run roughly a third above the national norm, and the gap between a prime street and one block over can be just as large as the gap between cities." Specificity (the prime-vs-side-street gap) is the surprising, useful part: lead with it.
- **Best visual (T4):** A bare index number is deliberately *not* a chart (so it can't be mis-read as a rent quote), which is defensible. But the section is so thin it barely earns a card. Better: fold the cost-of-living/space character into §3 customer (the wallet) OR into the hero's "what moves the score" so the page tells a tighter "deep demand vs expensive ground" story in one band. If kept standalone, the best visual is a small two-ended "national norm 100 — this city 132 — prime street ~180" mini-scale that conveys the *intra-city spread* (the actual insight), explicitly labelled relative.
- **Redundancy (T5):** Conceptually overlaps the hero caption ("expensive ground") and the honest-take ("rents take the upside back"). Three places say "London is expensive." **Depth (T6):** thin. **Honesty (T7):** honest framing.
- **VERDICT: REFORM or MERGE.** Reform into a sharper "cost of the ground" read that leads with the prime-vs-side-street spread (a real decision input), OR merge the single index into §3 so the page has one "who/what it costs to reach the customer" band instead of two thin ones.

### §5 Tourist money vs local money (visitor split)
- **Job (T2):** Tell the operator whether they're building for residents (steady, everyday) or tourists (seasonal, centre-only). P1, P5.
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Useful *as a character read* but at real risk of being slop because a single "Residents 72% / Visitors 28%" bar is one fact dressed as a section. The decision-relevant insight isn't the split, it's the *implication* (a tourist-heavy site is seasonal and footfall-dependent; a residents' city rewards loyalty). The current bar undersells that.
- **Cringe check (T3):** Big mis-read risk (the spec flags it): readers will assume 72% = revenue. The "footfall not spend" note is load-bearing and must stay. Sharpen the section by making the headline carry the *operator implication* ("Mostly a residents' city, so build for repeat trade, not a one-time tourist hit") rather than just stating the split.
- **Best visual (T4):** One proportion bar is correct and pies are rightly banned. But one bar = thin. Better option: keep the bar but add a *seasonality* read (peak vs trough months) which is the genuinely useful, surprising thing about tourist-dependence and would justify the section's existence. If no seasonality data is holdable, this section is a candidate to merge into the customer band.
- **Redundancy (T5):** Thematically adjacent to §3 customer (both describe "who walks through the door"). Could merge into a single "Your customer here" band: who they are (income spread), residents-vs-visitors, and seasonality. **Depth (T6):** currently shallow. **Honesty (T7):** the footfall-proxy framing is honest and essential.
- **VERDICT: REFORM (add seasonality / operator implication) or MERGE into §3 as a "Your customer" band.** A single bar alone does not earn a full section.

### §6 What owners keep across trades
- **Job (T2):** The payoff: show, for real, what owners *actually take home* across the everyday trades in this metro, with a difficulty read and a link to the full P&L. P1 (which trade?), P2 (benchmark to value a target), P3/P4 (like-for-like). This is the most monetizable section.
- **Useful or slop (T1):** The single most useful section on the page after the score. Real cell-engine numbers, ranked by take-home, with a break-in chip and a click-through to the cell. This is decision-grade.
- **Cringe check (T3):** The danger is the reader treating it as "dental beats cafe, so open a dental practice", which is a forbidden cross-trade ranking. The caveat ("ordered by take-home, not by what is best; read each on its own terms") is essential and must never be dropped. Sharpen: the bar should visually *not* look like a leaderboard; consider ordering or a visual treatment that says "these are different games" rather than "rank."
- **Best visual (T4):** Ranked bars (take-home) + tabular margin column + break-in chip is strong. One concern: the bar encodes take-home while a separate column shows margin, which can confuse (why is the long bar a low-margin trade?). Debated alternative: a small scatter/quadrant of margin vs take-home (rejected: too analyst-y for P1/P5). Better refinement: keep the bars but add a tiny inline "what you'd need to start" or "revenue to clear this" cue so the take-home number has a denominator. The chip linking to the cell is exactly right.
- **Redundancy (T5):** This is the city-level cousin of the country page's per-trade view and the cell's deep P&L; the relationship is correct (city = the menu, cell = the meal). No harmful overlap. **Depth (T6):** could add a "typical" vs "top-quartile" range per trade to avoid implying a single deterministic number. **Honesty (T7):** real, trusted-local only, self-omits below three rows: the gold standard for the page.
- **VERDICT: KEEP.** (Reform: give take-home a denominator/range so it doesn't read as a single guaranteed figure; keep the anti-ranking caveat sacrosanct.)

### §7 Best areas to set up
- **Job (T2):** Translate the city verdict into *action*: which district suits which trade, with a rent-vs-footfall trade-off. P1 (where do I actually sign a lease?).
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Potentially the most *actionable* section for P1, and the founder rightly flagged it for reinvention. The recommended Treatment 1 (suits shortlist with rent/footfall mini-meters) adds a real decision input. As currently mocked (district / suits / why, no meters) it's useful but underbuilt.
- **Cringe check (T3):** Risk of reading as generic local-guide copy ("Soho is trendy and expensive"). The rent/footfall pips are the antidote: they make it a *trade-off scan* ("high rent but high footfall" vs "mid rent, weekday-only"). Build the meters; the plain list is the floor, not the ship.
- **Best visual (T4):** Treatment 1 (divided list + suits pictogram + 4-segment rent/footfall pips) is the right call. The heat-grid matrix (Treatment 2) is denser but reads as a fabricated-precision scorecard and cramps at 375. Card grid (Treatment 3) clones §8/§10 and violates the no-repeated-grids rule. Treatment 1 wins. The pips must be explicitly "relative within this city."
- **Redundancy (T5):** Overlaps §8 neighbourhoods (both are "districts"). The distinction must be sharp: §7 = *prescriptive* (which area for which trade, a decision tool), §8 = *exploratory* (browse districts). If that distinction blurs, merge them. **Depth (T6):** the meters add the depth. **Honesty (T7):** London-exemplar only, SectionEmpty elsewhere, relative reads: honest.
- **VERDICT: REFORM (build the rent/footfall mini-meters; ship Treatment 1, not the bare list). Watch the §8 overlap.**

### §8 Neighbourhoods
- **Job (T2):** Hand off to the deeper neighbourhood pages: browse the districts. P1 (explore), P5 (curiosity), SEO (internal links).
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Useful as *navigation* and SEO, but as *content* it overlaps §7 heavily, and four cover cards with a character line is thin on its own merits. Its real job is link equity and "go deeper," which is legitimate but not a content section.
- **Cringe check (T3):** Risk of feeling like a directory stub. Make each card carry one *distinguishing* fact (a prime street + a character word that isn't a cliché) so it's a teaser, not a placeholder.
- **Best visual (T4):** Cover cards are fine for a navigational grid. The concern is three card-grid-ish sections (§7 list, §8 cards, §10 cards) clustering. The "no repeated grids in a row" rule handles it, but §7 and §8 being adjacent and both about districts is the real redundancy.
- **Redundancy (T5):** Strong overlap with §7. **Recommendation: merge §7 and §8** into one "Where to set up" band: the prescriptive suits-shortlist *is* the list of featured neighbourhoods, each row linking to its neighbourhood page. One band, both jobs (decision + navigation), no duplicate district treatment. **Depth (T6):** thin standalone. **Honesty (T7):** real data, fine.
- **VERDICT: MERGE with §7** (the suits-shortlist rows become the neighbourhood links; "Explore all neighbourhoods" stays as the tail link).

### §9 How the city is changing
- **Job (T2):** Forward-looking signal: what trend should an operator bet with/against (e.g. weekday office trade falling, evening/weekend rising). P1 (timing/positioning), P3 (thesis).
- **Useful or slop (T1):** When real, this is *premium* content (the contrarian, forward read nobody else gives). When London-exemplar-only and invented, it's the highest fabrication risk on the page: a confident trend claim that's actually a guess. Useful *if* defensible; slop and dangerous if not.
- **Cringe check (T3):** The "office week flattening" insight is genuinely good and specific. The risk is every city getting a generic "it's becoming more mixed-use" trend. Rule: only render where there's a *real, citable shift*; never invent a trend for a thin city (the spec already self-omits, good).
- **Best visual (T4):** Insight card + up/down rail is right; a chart would over-claim precision on a soft trend. The up/down arrows give it scannable structure. Correct.
- **Redundancy (T5):** None. **Depth (T6):** strong when filled. **Honesty (T7):** the most fragile section. Keep it London-exemplar-only and hold an explicit "our read, not a forecast" register. Consider demoting it below peers so an invented trend never sits high on a thin page.
- **VERDICT: KEEP** (with the honesty leash tight: real shifts only, "our read" register, self-omit aggressively).

### §10 Rival and peer cities
- **Job (T2):** Answer "compared to what?" and offer lateral moves: rank this metro against true peers on the one shared scale. P1 (is there a better nearby option?), P3/P4 (benchmark set).
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Useful and the *one legitimate place on the site to rank*, because it's cities-vs-cities on the identical scale. The "step sideways" cards drive exploration. Strong.
- **Cringe check (T3):** Risk of redundancy *with itself*: it currently carries THREE peer visuals (ComparisonBars, VsWorld median strip, and cover cards) all saying nearly the same thing. That's over-engineered and reads as padding. Sharpen by cutting to two: the ranked bars (the comparison) + the cards (the lateral nav). The VsWorld "vs peer median" strip is the redundant third.
- **Best visual (T4):** ComparisonBars (ranked, on 0-100) is the best comparison visual. Cover cards are the best nav visual. VsWorld median strip is a *third* take on the same data and adds little the bars don't already show (you can see where the subject sits in the ranked list). Recommend: keep bars + cards, **cut VsWorld** here.
- **Redundancy (T5):** Internal triple-redundancy (see above); also partial overlap with the hero's peer ticks, but that's the intended glance-vs-drilldown split. **Depth (T6):** good. **Honesty (T7):** the caveat rail is load-bearing and correct ("only cities are scored," "not adjusted for local prices").
- **VERDICT: REFORM (cut to ranked bars + step-sideways cards; drop the VsWorld median strip as redundant).**

### §11 One thing to remember
- **Job (T2):** The warm last word: the single sentence the operator should carry away, plus freshness + flag-it. P1, P5.
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Useful as a close and a trust signal (freshness stamp + "flag it" = we stand behind this and invite correction). Cheap, on-brand, good rhythm.
- **Cringe check (T3):** Risk of restating the hero/honest-take. The London line ("London gives you the demand; whether you keep it depends on the rent deal and the price you can hold") is distinct and earns its place. Keep each city's close *non-redundant* with its own §2.
- **Best visual (T4):** A quiet close card is right; no visual needed. Correct.
- **Redundancy (T5):** Overlaps §2 in *kind* but not content; acceptable as the bookend. **Depth (T6):** appropriately light. **Honesty (T7):** freshness + flag-it is exactly the honesty posture.
- **VERDICT: KEEP.**

### Collapse-behaviour strip (thin-city)
- **Job (T2):** Keep the IA locked and the page calm when a city is under-filled. Internal/structural, serves all by avoiding a skeleton-wall.
- **Useful or slop (T1):** Smart and necessary; it's the honest answer to "we don't have this yet" without faking data. Not a content section, a degradation pattern.
- **VERDICT: KEEP** (it's a mechanism, not a section; correct as designed).

## What is MISSING, new sections to ADD

1. **Competition / saturation read ("how crowded is the field").** The biggest gap. P1's first instinct is "how many [my trade] already exist here, and is the market saturated or under-served?" The page tells you what owners *keep* (§6) but nothing about how *contested* the market is. **Job:** show whether a trade is crowded or has white space in this metro. **Persona:** P1 (the make-or-break question), P3 (market structure). **Best visual:** a small "businesses per 10k residents vs the national norm" read per trade, or a simple over-/under-supplied chip on the §6 rows. Honesty leash: frame as a density character, not a precise count, and never as a cross-city ranking.

2. **Startup cost / capital-to-open band.** §6 tells you what you'd *keep* but never what you must *put in*. The hero says "if you can fund a slow start" but the page never quantifies the slow start. **Job:** rough capital and runway needed to open and survive the ramp. **Persona:** P1 (can I afford this?), P2 (deal sizing). **Best visual:** a small range strip ("typical money to open" low-mid-high) or a "months to break even" line, explicitly modeled. This directly serves the hero's own thesis and is currently a dangling promise.

3. **Survival / risk read ("can you survive the slow start").** The persona brief literally asks "can I survive the slow start" and the page never answers it. **Job:** a candid read on failure risk / how long the ramp is here. **Persona:** P1. **Best visual:** a "months to break even" or "share that make it past year X" character read, modeled and clearly labelled, paired with the honest-take. Even a qualitative "long ramp, high fixed costs" read would close the gap.

4. **Regulatory / friction read ("how hard to actually open").** The break-in chips on §6 hint at difficulty but the page never explains *why* it's hard (licensing, permits, labour cost, lease terms). **Job:** the practical friction of starting here. **Persona:** P1, P4. **Best visual:** a short 2-3 line "what makes it hard / what makes it easy" rail (same up/down idiom as §9). Honesty: qualitative, metro-specific, no invented scores.

5. **Cost-adjusted / real-spending-power read.** The VsWorld note admits the score is "not adjusted for local prices." That admission begs a section: a high salary in an expensive city isn't a rich customer. **Job:** translate nominal income/score into real local buying power. **Persona:** P1, P3. **Best visual:** could be folded into §3 (customer) as a "what that income buys here" line, paired with the cost character from §4, turning two thin sections into one sharp one.

## What to CUT or MERGE

- **CUT the optional re-skinned radial (§1).** A radial adds no peer context the ScoreBand already gives and risks reading as a vanity dial. The big number + band is complete. Drop it to keep the hero clean.
- **CUT the VsWorld peer-median strip (§10).** It's a third visual on the same peer data; the ranked bars already show where the subject sits relative to peers. Redundant; remove for a tighter peers band.
- **MERGE §8 neighbourhoods into §7 best-areas.** Both are "districts." Combine into one prescriptive-and-navigational "Where to set up" band where each suits-shortlist row links to its neighbourhood page, with an "Explore all" tail. Eliminates the page's clearest content redundancy and one of three card-ish grids.
- **MERGE or sharply REFORM §4 space + §5 visitors into the §3 customer band.** Three thin sections (income spread, cost character, residents-vs-visitors) all describe "the market you're selling into." Fused into one "Your customer and the cost to reach them" band (income spread + real spending power + residents/visitors/seasonality), they become one substantial, non-repetitive band instead of three shallow ones, and the page gains room for the missing competition/startup-cost sections without bloating.

## The reformed, re-ordered section list (final recommendation)

1. **Hero + Business Climate Score** — the one-number verdict + sentence + ScoreBand with peer ticks + three context stats; caption always names the core tension. *Visual: big Newsreader number + 0-100 ScoreBand with peer ticks (no radial).*
2. **The honest take** — the editorial conscience; metro-specific, falsifiable bullets only. *Visual: calm accent panel, verdict line + 3 bullets.*
3. **Your customer and the cost to reach them** (merge of §3 + §4 + §5) — who the wallet is (income spread), what that income really buys here (cost-adjusted read), and residents-vs-visitors/seasonality, each with a "so what" line. *Visual: RangeStrip (income spread) as the hero of the band + a small relative cost scale + the one proportion bar; one band, three honest reads.*
4. **What owners keep across trades** — the monetizable payoff: real take-home, margin, break-in chip, cell link; anti-ranking caveat sacrosanct; give take-home a denominator/range. *Visual: ranked take-home bars + tabular margin + break-in chip per row.*
5. **What it takes to open here** (NEW) — capital-to-open range and months-to-break-even, answering the hero's own "if you can fund a slow start" promise. *Visual: a "money to open" range strip + a "months to break even" line, modeled.*
6. **How crowded the field is** (NEW) — saturation/white-space read per trade, the make-or-break market-structure question. *Visual: per-trade density-vs-norm chip on the §4 rows or a small over-/under-supplied bar.*
7. **Where to set up** (merge of §7 + §8) — the prescriptive suits-shortlist with rent/footfall mini-meters, each row linking to its neighbourhood page; "Explore all neighbourhoods" tail. *Visual: divided list + suits pictogram + 4-segment rent/footfall pips (Treatment 1).*
8. **How the city is changing** — forward read, real shifts only, "our read not a forecast" register; demoted below the action sections so an invented trend never sits high. *Visual: insight card + up/down rail.*
9. **Rival and peer cities** — the one legitimate ranking (cities-vs-cities on the shared scale) + step-sideways nav; VsWorld dropped. *Visual: ranked ComparisonBars + peer cover cards.*
10. **One thing to remember** — the warm, non-redundant last word + freshness stamp + flag-it. *Visual: quiet close card.*

Plus the existing **collapse-to-one-strip** degradation for thin cities, applied across the reformed spine, and the chrome (navbar, breadcrumb, footer) at the boundaries.

The net effect: three thin "market" sections fuse into one substantial customer band; two duplicate district sections fuse into one action band; two redundant visuals (radial, VsWorld) are cut; and the freed room funds the three sections the persona brief explicitly asks for and the page currently lacks (cost to open, survival/ramp, competition). The page goes from "verdict + almanac facts" to "verdict + everything a P1 needs to decide and act," without adding length or weakening a single honesty guardrail.
