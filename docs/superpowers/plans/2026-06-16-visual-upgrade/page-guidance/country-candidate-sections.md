# Country page , candidate new sections

## The curated menu (grouped by theme)

Curation principle: four lenses produced ~47 candidates that collapse into ~16 distinct sections. Many "new" ideas already live on the spine (the constitution lists 24 sections, not the dozen named in the brief). I treat those as covered and only surface what is genuinely missing. Graphic budget is tracked against the whole page so no type appears more than twice.

### Movement A , What it actually costs to run (Cost)

**1. The real cost to keep the doors open**
- Role: the recurring monthly burn (rent, energy, rates, connectivity, insurance, payroll floor) , the runway-killer no one quotes as one figure. Modeled national bar shown beside the London-UK exemplar bar for calibration.
- Graphic: **cost-stack bar** (use 1 of 2)
- **MUST-ADD**

**2. The all-in tax an operator actually feels**
- Role: stacks corporate tax + employer social contributions + other levies into the effective load on a stated example profit, with take-home as the remainder. Kills the Googleable headline rate.
- Graphic: **waterfall** (use 1 of 2)
- **STRONG**

**3. Getting a roof: buy, lease, or serviced**
- Role: what a landlord demands before keys , deposit months, lease length, break clauses, who pays repairs, exit penalty. The biggest blind commitment a first-timer signs.
- Graphic: **comparison table** (use 1 of 2; "Versus the neighbours" is the other)
- **STRONG**

### Movement B , Getting in and getting legal (Entry / Path-in)

**4. Red tape, in order, with the wait**
- Role: the launch critical path , register, tax ID, bank account, VAT, permits , each step with typical elapsed days and the known bottleneck flagged (usually the foreign-owned bank account). The formation tile gives cost, not sequence and wait.
- Graphic: **flow / step timeline** (use 1 of 2)
- **MUST-ADD**

**5. Can a foreign-owned business actually bank here**
- Role: the silent killer of cross-border launches , entities form in days but stay un-bankable for months without local directors / residency / in-person KYC. Changes whether you need a resident director or an EMI workaround.
- Graphic: **gauge** friction meter + a short requirement checklist (use 1 of 2)
- **STRONG**

**6. The cover you can't trade without**
- Role: mandatory vs strongly-advised insurance/compliance (employer liability, public liability, premises) , some illegal to skip , with the rough annual bill. First-timers under-insure and find out the hard way.
- Graphic: **bullet bar** (each cover: bar + "mandatory" threshold marker + typical cost) (use 1 of 2)
- **OPTIONAL**

### Movement C , Cash, demand and the calendar (Reward / Demand)

**7. When the money actually comes in**
- Role: late-payment culture , agreed terms vs actual days-to-paid. Decides whether you need a buffer or a factoring line from day one. Distinct from "who has money" (that is spending power, this is cash timing).
- Graphic: **dumbbell** (use 1 of 2)
- **MUST-ADD**

**8. The trading year, month by month**
- Role: ONE merged calendar that does double duty , statutory filing/tax/renewal months (real, factual) with demand-seasonality intensity shaded behind them. Drives hiring timing, cash buffers, launch date.
- Graphic: **calendar / heatmap** (use 1 of 2)
- **MUST-ADD**

**9. Who your customers are: the household budget**
- Role: the allocation of a typical household budget (housing / food / leisure / services) with the discretionary slice highlighted , does your category have wallet room. A real demand read, not "people spend a lot." (Note: overlaps the existing "Who has money to spend" tile , see cut list; keep only if that tile is spending-power-level, not budget-share.)
- Graphic: **proportion bar** (use 1 of 2)
- **STRONG** (conditional , de-dupe against existing #8)

### Movement D , The competitive field and the economy's shape (Demand / Edge)

**10. Where the money and the jobs are**
- Role: the shape of the economy , sectors by share of output, each tile sub-split by jobs density. Tells a prospective owner whether their B2B demand, foot traffic and talent pool exist. You cannot Google "is this a good country for a B2B services firm."
- Graphic: **treemap** (use 1 of 2)
- **MUST-ADD**

**11. How crowded is the field**
- Role: firms-per-capita density shaded by independent-vs-chain concentration , do you win on price, quality or capital. Distinct from "five common trades" (ease-to-open) and the opportunity gap (thin trades).
- Graphic: **dot-grid / waffle** (use 1 of 2)
- **STRONG**

**12. Which sectors are pulling ahead**
- Role: direction beats level , each sector's earlier year vs latest, so growth/decline reads by line length. Genuinely hard to assemble yourself. (Shares the dumbbell with #7 , this is the second and final dumbbell.)
- Graphic: **dumbbell** (use 2 of 2)
- **OPTIONAL** (cut if a third dumbbell-worthy idea is preferred)

### Movement E , Will the ground hold, and can you get out (Risk / Path-out)

**13. The risk picture, scored by axis**
- Role: the components a lender underwrites , currency volatility, inflation drift, payment-default, contract-enforcement , each dot vs a named peer-group median. Shows which axis to hedge, not a verdict. Sharpens the existing "ground under you" tile from vibe to axes.
- Graphic: **ranked / bullet bars vs peer median** , RESOLVED to **ranked bars** to avoid a third bullet bar (use 1 of 2)
- **STRONG**

**14. Currency you actually get paid in**
- Role: multi-year local-currency vs USD/EUR with a shaded volatility band , the FX drift that erodes a multi-year hold. No forecast line (that would be a fabricated number).
- Graphic: **area trend** (use 1 of 2)
- **STRONG**

**15. The exit climate for a small business**
- Role: nearly every country page tells you how to get IN; this answers whether you can get OUT , buyer depth, typical small-business sale multiple, time-to-sell. Closes the Path lens. Marked indicative or "not enough reliable data" rather than inventing a multiple.
- Graphic: **gauge** (use 2 of 2) , liquidity read + typical-multiple label
- **MUST-ADD**

**16. What will surprise a developed-market operator**
- Role: the 4-6 genuine deltas vs an explicitly-defined generic baseline (a tax quirk, a hiring norm, a payment habit, a licensing trap). Serves the operator who assumes "another Western market" and gets burned. Editorial-tagged.
- Graphic: **area trend** , RESOLVED: reuse the small-multiples **area trend** is taken; assign **proportion / delta strip** rendered as a horizontal **diverging bar** (a ranked-bar variant) , flagged below.
- **OPTIONAL**

## What I cut, and why

- **Versus the world / generic-baseline-as-its-own-section** , already CUT from the page; do not reintroduce.
- **What actually closes businesses (failure causes)** , overlaps the existing "survival odds"; fold causes INTO that tile, not a new section.
- **The cost ladder / cost-vs-ease positioning scatter** , overlaps "Versus the neighbours"; risks ranking-across-geography; cut.
- **The licences your trade needs (dot-grid matrix)** , the spine already has a Licences section; fold the trade-x-permit detail there.
- **Your first 90 days / setup clock (Gantt swimlanes)** , duplicates "Red tape in order" (#4); one timeline only , keep the critical-path version, cut the Gantt.
- **How owners fund the start (funding-mix proportion bar)** , thin and modeled-heavy; "access to finance: medium" risk; the financing-climate idea is stronger , cut the funding-mix.
- **The financing climate (borrow cost + equity availability)** , decision-grade but the SMB-spread is heavily modeled and overlaps #13's currency/rates axis; cut to protect honesty, OPTIONAL revival later.
- **Doing business in English (dumbbell)** , real but folds cleanly into the existing "talent reality"; not its own section.
- **Three ways in (own entity / partner / franchise)** , strong, but overlaps the existing "special zones and structures" and formation tiers; hold as OPTIONAL, do not crowd Movement B.
- **Selling across the border (import duty/VAT waterfall)** , niche to physical-supply-chain operators; second waterfall slot is better spent on the universal tax load (#2); cut.
- **Can goods and people move (infrastructure bullet bars)** , real and good, but the bullet-bar budget is spent on #6; demote to OPTIONAL backlog.
- **Open to outsiders / trade-partner mini map** , a choropleth is visually unique but the content is largely Googleable (top trade partners) and skews macro-trivia; cut, salvage the foreign-ownership rule into "special zones."
- **The shock log (multi-year event heatmap)** , the calendar/heatmap budget is spent on #8; honesty-risky to populate per country; cut.
- **Talent depth + churn (ranked bars)** , folds into existing "talent reality"; not standalone.
- **Stability small-multiples (inflation/currency/rates)** , collapsed INTO #13 + #14 so it isn't a third trend cluster.
- **The three-year outlook gauge** , overlaps the existing "honest take / gut-check" close AND the gauge budget (#5, #15); cut , the close already synthesizes.
- **Cost-competitiveness proportion bar (investor)** , duplicate of #1's cost stack + #9's proportion bar; cut.

**Shared-graphic conflicts resolved:** dumbbell would have appeared 3x (#7 payments, #12 sectors, plus English/momentum) , kept to 2 (#7, #12). Gauge would appear 3x (#5 bank, #15 exit, outlook) , kept to 2 (#5, #15), outlook cut. Bullet bar would appear 3x (#6 cover, #13 risk, infrastructure) , #13 moved to ranked bars, infrastructure cut. Calendar/heatmap kept to 1 (merged filing+seasonality), shock-log cut. Waterfall kept to 1 (#2 tax), import-duty cut. #16 reassigned to a diverging-bar so area-trend stays at 1 (#14).

## My top 8 recommendations to add first

Ranked by decision-impact x universality x graphic distinctiveness, each a different graphic:

1. **The real cost to keep the doors open** (cost-stack bar) , the single most-asked, runway-defining number.
2. **Red tape, in order, with the wait** (flow timeline) , turns the formation tile into a schedulable launch plan; universal.
3. **Where the money and the jobs are** (treemap) , the economy's shape; tells an owner if their demand and talent exist; visually unique.
4. **The trading year, month by month** (calendar/heatmap) , merges real filing deadlines + seasonality; drives hiring, cash, launch date.
5. **When the money actually comes in** (dumbbell) , late-payment culture; a day-one cash-flow decision few think to ask.
6. **The exit climate for a small business** (gauge) , closes the Path lens; almost no country page answers "can I get out."
7. **The all-in tax an operator actually feels** (waterfall) , kills the misleading headline rate with the effective load.
8. **Can a foreign-owned business actually bank here** (gauge) , the silent cross-border launch killer; changes the entity structure decision.

Honesty floor for all eight: UK shown as the real exemplar; every other country's modeled component is tagged; cities remain the only scored entity; nothing ranks across business x geography.
