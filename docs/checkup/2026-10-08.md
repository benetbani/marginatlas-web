## Verdict

The site is in better structural health than it was two days ago, and the machinery that keeps it honest works; the paid
layer is built well but built narrow. Billing, checkout, the portal, the session and the server-side paywall are real,
tested and switched off, and none of the findings below blocks launch day as he ruled it (one plan, UK depth); the one
blocker before the paywall is already queued (uk:trade-pages-by-country: a UK trade page outside the seven cities prints
world-typical figures under lines that never say whose, and the lock would sell it). What blocks the product he now
describes is a decision, not a fault: everything that knows "Pro" knows it as one bit (13 files spell the tier as a
literal, the database checks `free` or `pro`), the paywall sells the UK only (/gb, 7 of 252 city pages, the UK trade
pages), and what Pro opens is declared in three places that share no list. Two tiers, an API and an MCP server all need
one capability map first. On the data, 683 of the 867 figures the ten main renders print (78.8%) carry no stamp saying
where they came from, and no instrument counts that across the site: for a source people pay for, that stamp is the
product. The cleanup found and fixed real faults: four scripts hid from search and review behind control bytes (one gate
clause could never fire), the layering allowlist grandfathered three deleted paths, the entry documents typed a "~60s"
chain that takes 1,018.6 s here and 25 gates where there are 264, and every gate paid one to three seconds of npx start
(twenty quick gates: 45.7 s to 19.4 s on average). The branch ends with the whole chain green, 265 of 265 in 817.2 s, and
the full typecheck clean.
