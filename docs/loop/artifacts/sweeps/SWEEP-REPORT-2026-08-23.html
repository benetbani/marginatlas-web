Six sweeps across the whole site

These look for the faults that reading one section at a time cannot see. That pass reads a single surface per turn, on purpose, so anything that only shows up across a whole page, or between two pages, has been found by accident until now. Four such faults were one glance away from shipping.

The two findings worth your time are the first and the third.

Just under half the sections on the site reach nobody , 23 of 48. Not broken: they correctly hide themselves because the numbers behind them were never sourced. But it means a reader gets a much thinner page than the design describes, and it tells me six of the sections still on my list need no work at all.

The pay ladder is printed twice, one block directly above the other, on every trade page. The note in that card says removing the repeat would cost a figure. That was true of the bundled sample, which has five pay roles. Every real page has three, so the two blocks are word for word identical.

Every list below is candidates, not verdicts, and each sweep states in its own words what it cannot tell apart. Four carry a calibration: they are pointed at a fault already confirmed by hand, and if they miss it their zeroes mean nothing. Every one of the six needed its first result thrown away , the first draft of a sweep is always too forgiving, and one of them reported a reassuring "one file" where the honest answer was fifteen.

1. Sections that reach no reader

23 of the 48 sections in the site reach none of the fifteen real pages I rendered. Just under half. A card whose figures are dropped before the page is built omits itself, which is correct behaviour; the point is how many do it. Eleven of these had been found one at a time, an iteration each. This found the rest in one run, including six that are open items on my list and six that were never on it at all.

4. The same claim disagreeing between two pages

Nothing, and the reason is the result. Of fifteen pages, only two places are described by more than one page. Zero claims disagree, zero agree, and eight could not be compared because only one page prints them. The pages cannot contradict each other because they never make the same claim twice. Kept as an early warning for when more sections start reaching readers.

  15 pages rendered.
  2 place(s) described by two or more pages, which is the only
  situation in which a like-for-like comparison exists at all:
    london       city/London, cell/london/restaurants, cell/london/hair-salons, hood/london
    new-york     city/New York, cell/new-york/restaurants

  0 place-level claim(s) that DISAGREE between two pages about the same place
  0 that appear on two such pages and AGREE
  8 where fewer than two pages print it, so nothing could be compared

  Read those three together. A zero on the first line means nothing on its own:
  when the third is large, the claims simply never meet, and that is the more
  likely reading on this site because half its sections reach no reader.

6. Drawings that stretch their own geometry

Zero, and that one is finished. Of 71 fixed-size drawings, 51 are icons that are meant to scale, one is a full-bleed cover, and not one stretches a measurement. Three charts were rebuilt during this work for exactly this fault and there are none left.

  71 drawing(s) with a fixed viewBox across the components
  51 of them are icon-sized and are meant to scale with their type
  1 are full-width AND carry a fixed height, so only the scale stretches
  0 are full-width with NO fixed height, so the whole drawing stretches


  A drawing that carries a measurement should not scale its own geometry.
  A decorative one may. Open each.
