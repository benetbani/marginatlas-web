# Forward architecture: founder decisions + design vision (2026-06-14)

The founder answered a 40-question elicitation on the forward content/section architecture, then
added a major design-direction layer. This record is the authoritative input for the Part 9 / 10
plan. Architecture first (an agent fills the content blanks later); the founder stressed the
architecture is of the utmost importance right now.

## The 40 decisions

### A. Page spine and element order
1. Spine: **per-type spines** (each page type keeps its own spine; only the masthead is shared).
2. Above the fold: **answer + number + spread** (the signature trio).
3. Honest-take: **first**, right after the masthead.
4. The "one thing to remember": **pinned at the close**.
5. Motion: **subtle fade/slide reveal** per section, reduced-motion safe.
6. Density: **lean core + "more"** (answer + ~5 core sections, the deep stuff behind expand).

### B. Switchers + the make-it-yours calculator
7. Sub-type switch: **in the masthead title**.
8. Switch scope: **the whole page re-derives** (numbers + read).
9. Venue context (high-street/mall/airport/station): **a global switcher** beside sub-type.
10. Calculator placement: **inline on the spread** (drag your "you" marker).
11. Calculator levers: **a few high-impact** (rent, staff, your draw).
12. Calculator persistence: **a light profile follows you** across pages.

### C. Personalization + decision flow
13. "You" context: **an optional light profile chip**.
14. Decide flow: **a first-class guided path** (trade to place to number wizard).
15. Save/watch: **a global watch/compare tray** that accumulates.
16. Add-to-compare: **on every page** (feeds the tray).
17. Dashboard: **a /you page** aggregating saved + profile.

### D. Cross-altitude navigation
18. Altitude nav: **a persistent zoom control** (keep trade, change place-altitude).
19. Coordinates: **both trade and place are sticky** as you navigate.
20. Cross-link rail: **same trade nearby + same place other trades**.
21. "vs the world" anchor: **the global median** for the trade (one fixed anchor).

## The design-direction vision (founder, in their words, paraphrased)

- **Functional = the user feels in control.** Functional does not mean unaesthetic. A page design
  that makes the user feel in control of the thing. Extremely important.
- **A design-improvement pipeline.** The founder wants to move forward with a pipeline / operating
  model for how the design is continuously improved, using the best design methodologies.
- **Digestibility of dense, factual pages.** Atlas pages are by nature very dense in information;
  the central design problem is making that density digestible.
- **Overcome the "coldness"** of factual, dense data. Not only via the terracotta/brick + cream
  palette, but via design choices that make things "sweeter and more transparent."
- **Glassmorphism.** The founder is in favour of glassmorphic design, with a rich background, to
  make the surface beautiful and warm.
- **Beautiful, category-specific backgrounds.** Cities get beautiful city backgrounds; countries
  get beautiful country backgrounds; businesses are a different category of background.
- **Backgrounds behind the whole page, especially the side gutters.** A plain white page reads
  cold/dense. Idea: put the rich background on the left and right edges (the gutters) so the page
  feels less cold; the content column stays readable.
- **Static background, scrolling content.** The image can stay fixed/static at the back while the
  text scrolls over it. (A fixed-attachment background.)
- **Heavy research required first.** The founder explicitly wants heavy research on the potential
  and functionality of all this (glassmorphism, the backgrounds, digestibility, in-control design,
  the pipeline) and how it rolls out, before building.
