The honest take

The headline revenue is real, but a London restaurant is a wages-and-rent business, not a high-margin one.

  • Rent in London takes a bigger bite here than almost anywhere else in the country.
  • Skilled staff are hard to keep and the wage floor keeps rising.
  • Pricing power is real, customers will pay up for the right room, which is the lever that makes the model work.
  • Demand leans on local residents, office workers, tourists, so the site and the footfall matter as much as the food.

What to watch
  • Rent resets on renewal
    A lease step-up at review can take a year's margin overnight. Lock the longest term you can and budget for the jump before it lands.
    Serious
  • Holding good staff
    Skilled people are hard to keep and the wage floor keeps climbing, so payroll rarely sits still and a key departure stings.
    Watch
  • A quiet stretch in the calendar
    Trade leans on the warmer months and December, so a slow January and February can undo a thin-margin autumn.
    Watch
  • A supplier or energy shock
    Input and energy prices can jump with little warning. It does not happen often, but on this margin it bites hard when it does.

What locals know
  • The good sites change hands quietly, before they ever reach an agent's window.
  • Rent-free fit-out periods are negotiable; the headline rent rarely is.
  • Chains hold about 28 in 100 of the market, so an independent competes on the thing they cannot copy.
  • Footfall on a street can halve one block over; walk it at the hour you would trade before you sign.
Against the grain

The second site, not the first, is where a London restaurant owner actually makes money.

The first pays for the learning. The brand, the suppliers, and the team only pay off when they are spread across more than one room.

Myth versus reality

What people get wrong

  • MythA great chef guarantees a great business.

    ReallyA great landlord deal does more for the bottom line than a star review.

  • MythHigher prices always mean higher profit.

    ReallyAbove the local ceiling, customers fall away faster than the margin rises.

Right for, wrong for

Who this business suits

A good fit if
  • You can fund a slow first year without drawing a wage
  • You can build and keep a skilled team
  • You can hold a premium position rather than chase volume
Think twice if
  • You need a salary from month one
  • You expect to run it without reliable staff
  • You are buying yourself a job, not a business
Gut check

Three questions before you sign a lease

  • 1Can you cover six months of rent and wages before the revenue arrives?
  • 2Are you confident of clearing 16 covers a day from the first season?
  • 3Would the numbers still work if takings came in a fifth under plan?
