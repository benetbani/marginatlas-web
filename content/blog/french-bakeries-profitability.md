Open the data on French bakeries (NAF code 10.71B, *Cuisson de produits de boulangerie*) and you see something almost unique in European retail: stable, modest profit, year after year.

The typical patisserie-boulangerie earns around **€280,000 in yearly revenue**, with about **three employees**, generating **~€85k in personnel costs**. That leaves roughly €40k–€60k of operating profit before owner's compensation.

It's not glamorous. But it's resilient. France lost only 4% of its bakeries between 2015 and 2020, a tighter contraction than restaurants, cafés, or specialty groceries.

What protects them? A combination of:

- Daily perishable goods → repeat customers locked in
- Fragmented competition (few chains, lots of independents)
- Strict tradition rules (an artisan baker must bake on-site)

Data from Eurostat sbs_sc_sca_r2.
