date: "2026-05-13"
excerpt: "The word 'small' means different things. A 50-person firm is a giant in some industries and a startup in others, and Germany and the US draw the lines very differently."
author: "Margin Atlas team"
---

Both Germany and the US officially classify businesses by employee count, but the cutoffs diverge in interesting places.

In the US, the Small Business Administration uses industry-specific thresholds: a 'small' manufacturer can have up to 1,500 employees. In Germany, the Mittelstand definition cuts at 500 people and €50M revenue.

What this means for benchmarking: a 250-employee firm in Germany sits at the *top* of the Mittelstand pile. The same firm in the US is well within the 'small' bracket for most industries.

This is why Margin Atlas uses harmonized size bands (1, 2-9, 10-49, 50-249, 250+) rather than each country's local definition. Same brackets everywhere makes cross-border comparison honest.
