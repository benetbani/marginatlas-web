The typical US pharmacy (NAICS 446110) brings in about $4.1 million per year. The typical French pharmacie about €1.9 million. The typical German Apotheke about €2.3 million.

What looks like a huge gap shrinks once you adjust for size: US pharmacies are bigger (median 8 employees) than French ones (median 4) and German ones (median 5).

### Per-employee economics

On a per-employee revenue basis, the three countries land much closer:

- US: $510k revenue / employee
- France: €475k revenue / employee
- Germany: €450k revenue / employee

Where they diverge is in the payroll share. French pharmacies spend ~38% of revenue on personnel costs. US pharmacies, ~22%. German pharmacies, ~32%.

The US gap mostly reflects scale: bigger pharmacies have lower payroll ratios as they spread fixed retail labor over more square feet.
